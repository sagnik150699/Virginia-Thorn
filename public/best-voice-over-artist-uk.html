Human vs AI

Why a real voice-over artist beats an AI voice

An AI voice can produce a first take in seconds, so it looks quicker and cheaper. The time comes back in editing, and this is where it goes.

Editing time

A generated read has to be checked line by line. Names, brands, places, acronyms and numbers are often mispronounced, so they are respelled or tagged and generated again, and each new take can change the delivery of the words around them. A professional voice-over artist checks how to say them before recording, reads them correctly and records pick-ups on request.

Pace and emphasis

Timing is a performance choice. A voice-over artist reads to picture, lands a 30-second spot on time, breathes with the sentence and stresses the word that sells the line. Generated speech tends to run at an even pace with the stress in the wrong place, and fixing it means cutting, re-timing and stitching clips by hand.

Direction

In a session you can ask for warmer, faster or a smile on the last line and hear it straight away. With a synthetic voice you change the text or the settings and generate again, with no guarantee that the next version is closer.

Consistency

Across a long e-learning course or an audiobook, a narrator keeps the same voice, energy and pronunciation from the first module to the last. Synthetic reads can drift from one generation to the next, so long projects need extra listening passes to catch it.

A tone that fits the brand

The Philips Hue brief asked for “a woman who is confident, playful & reassuring”. That is a character to play, and playing it is acting: it is what a professional voice-over artist is booked for.

Clear usage rights

With a human artist you agree where and for how long the recording will be used, and the voice belongs to a real, named person. A stock synthetic voice is open to anyone using the same software, so check its licence for commercial use and whether the same voice could turn up in someone else's advert.

The honest sum: a first take is quick to generate, but checking, correcting, regenerating and re-timing it often costs more time and money than booking a professional voice-over artist, and the result can still sound synthetic.
